IMPLEMENTATION  PLAN  GUIDELINES     
 
1. Introduction 
These guidelines outline the expectations for preparing the Implementation Plan for your ECE496 
capstone design project. You have already submitted a Proposal, which, much like in industry, 
serves as the document a client or sponsor reviews before approving a project. While the Proposal 
defines what the project is intended to achieve in terms of functionality, performance, and System 
Requirements Specification (SyRS), the Implementation Plan focuses on how the proposed 
solution will be designed, implemented, and verified. 
The Implementation Plan should demonstrate that your team has progressed from a proposed 
concept to a feasible engineering design that can be implemented and tested. It should provide a 
clear technical path for moving the project from concept to realization. 
 
 
2. The Implementation Plan 
The Implementation Plan presents the technical design of your proposed solution and explains how 
it will be implemented and verified. It should demonstrate the development of your project from 
the initial concept presented in the Proposal toward a detailed engineering design. Specifically, it 
should include: 
• System Block Diagram: An enhanced and more detailed version of the diagram presented 
in your Proposal. It should show the major functional modules and sub -blocks, with each 
clearly labeled according to its function. Connections between modules should identify 
their interfaces and, where appropriate, the flow of signals, data, or power. 
• Alternative Approaches: A discussion of three feasible design approaches, including the 
approach selected for implementation. Compare the approaches at the system level, discuss 
the important trade-offs, and justify why the selected approach is the most appropriate for 
meeting the project goals and requirements. 
• Detailed Requirements:  A refined and updated System Requirements Specification 
(SyRS) that incorporates feedback from the Proposal and any approved changes, while 
remaining consistent with the project goals and the selected design approach. 
• Verification Plan: A description of how each system requirement will be verified. The 
plan should identify the appropriate verification methods and define measurable 
acceptance criteria for determining whether each requirement has been satisfied. 
 
 
3. List of Sections 
The Implementation Plan should include the following sections. Consult your supervisor regarding 
any additional sections or information that may be required for your project. 
Cover Page (One page) 
Include the project title, section number, team members' names, supervisor's name, administrator's 
name, and date. 
I. Summary of Project Status and Changes (Max one page) 
Provide a concise update on the project since the Proposal, including progress made and any 
significant changes in scope, requirements, or design approach. 
II. Possible Solutions and Design Alternatives (Max three pages) 
Describe three feasible design approaches, including the approach selected for implementation. 
Compare the approaches at the system level, discuss the important trade-offs, and justify why the 
selected approach is the most appropriate for meeting the project goals and requirements.

III. Technical Design and Implementation (Max four pages) 
Provide the technical details of your selected design, including: 
• System-Level Overview: A brief explanation of the overall design and its principle of

• System Block Diagram: An expanded and more detailed version of the diagram presented

• Module Descriptions: A description of the func tion of each major module or sub -block,

IV. Test and Verification (Max two pages) 
Explain how the design will be tested and verified, including: 
• Verification Table:  Link each system requirem ent to its verification method, such as

V. Appendices 
The appendices are not subject to the page limits specified above. The followi ng appendices are 
required: 
Appendix A: System Requirements Specification (SyRS) 
Appendix B: Gantt Chart 
Appendix C: Financial Plan – Budget Table 
Additional appendices may be included as needed to provide supporting information, data, graphs, 
tables, or other material relevant to the project. 
 
Page Limits: The page limits specified above apply to the main sections of the Implementation 
Plan, including figures and tables. The cover page, references, and appendices are excluded from 
these limits. References should be provided in a separate section following Section IV and before 
the appendices. 
 
 
4. Descriptions of Document Sections 
I. Summary of Project Status and Changes 
This section provides a brief overview of your project's current status and progress since 
submitting the Proposal. It should address the following points: 
• Implementation Progress:  Summarize the technical work completed so far, including 
design decisions, simulations, prototyping, and implementation activities. Identify any 
modules or components that have been partially or fully implemented and indicate what is 
currently functional. 
• Challenges Encountered:  Describe any significant technical or implementation 
challenges encountered since submitting the Proposal and explain how you have addressed 
or plan to address them. 
• Plans Ahead: Outline the major tasks remaining and your plans for completing them in 
the coming weeks. 
In addition, describe any significant changes made to the project since the Proposal, including 
changes to the Project Goal, System Requirements, design approach, or Risk Assessment. Provide 
a brief justification for each change. Significant changes must be reviewed and approved by your 
supervisor.

II. Possible Solutions and Design Alternatives 
Describe three feasible design approaches considered by your team, including the approac h 
selected for implementation. The alternatives should represent meaningful system -level design 
choices rather than minor differences in coding, implementation details, or individual components. 
Compare the three approaches based on criteria relevant to yo ur project, such as performance, 
complexity, cost, reliability, implementation effort, available resources, and ability to meet the 
System Requirements. Discuss the advantages, limitations, and important trade -offs of each 
approach. 
Based on your comparison, justify why the selected approach is the most appropriate for meeting 
the project goals and requirements. Where appropriate, use a comparison table to summarize the 
alternatives and their trade-offs. 
 
III. Technical Design and Implementation 
This section des cribes the technical design of your selected solution and how it will be 
implemented. Break down the overall system into smaller functional modules or sub -blocks and 
explain how they work together. Include the following: 
• System-Level Overview:  Summarize th e overall design and describe its principle of 
operation, key algorithms, or process flow, as applicable. Explain the main technical 
approach and indicate whether it is based on established methods, modifications of existing 
designs, or new ideas. Where applicable, cite relevant references. 
• System Block Diagram:  Present an enhanced and more detailed version of the System 
Block Diagram provided in the Proposal. The diagram should identify the major functional 
modules and sub-blocks, their interconnections, a nd how they interact within the overall 
system. Clearly label the modules and relevant connections, including the flow of data, 
signals, or power, where applicable. Figures 1 and 2  provide examples of different System 
Block Diagrams. 
• Module Descriptions: Describe the function of each major module or sub-block shown in 
the System Block Diagram. Identify its important inputs and outputs, explain how it 
operates, and describe its interfaces with other modules. Where applicable, provide 
relevant technical details such as signals, data formats, communication protocols, voltage 
or current levels, mechanical interfaces, or APIs. Include important module -level 
specifications that affect the system's ability to meet its overall requirements. 
The level of detail should be sufficient to demonstrate that the major technical design decisions 
have been made and that the team has a clear plan for implementation, integration, and testing.

IV. Test and Verification  
Describe the testing and evaluation completed so far, and explain how you plan to verify that the 
final system meets its System Requirements (SyRS). Consider what evidence would convince a 
client—or the audience at the Design Fair—that your system performs as intended. 
Include the following, where applicable: 
• Verification Methods and Setups: Describe the methods and procedures used or planned 
for verification, including required equipment, diagnostic software, test setups, or special 
arrangements. 
• Sample Si zes and Test Conditions:  Specify the number of trials or samples, where 
applicable, and the operating conditions under which the tests will be performed.

• Acceptance Criteria:  Define measurable success criteria (pass/fail conditions) for 
determining whether each system requirement has been satisfied. 
Clearly distinguish between tests already completed and those planned for later stages of the 
project. Each verification activity should provide evidence that the system meets its specified 
requirements. 
 
Verification Table 
Provide a Verification Table that lists each System Requirement using the same requirement ID 
assigned in your SyRS. For each requirement, identify the verification method, briefly describe 
how verification will be performed, and specify measurable acceptance criteria. 
Possible verification methods include: 
• TEST: Direct measurement or testing of the system's performance under specified 
conditions. This is the most common verification method. 
• ANALYSIS: Use of mathematical calculations, sim ulations, or engineering analysis to 
demonstrate compliance with a requirement. 
• REVIEW OF DESIGN: Examination of design documents, specifications, drawings, or 
component information to confirm that a requirement has been satisfied. 
• SIMILARITY: Demonstration of compliance based on an existing design or component 
with sufficiently similar characteristics and previously verified performance. This method 
should be used only when the similarity can be justified and supported by evidence.

V. Required Appendices  
The following appendices are required. Additional appendices may be included as needed to 
provide supporting information relevant to the project. 
 
Appendix A: System Requirements Specification (SyRS) 
Provide a complete and updated System Requirements Specification (SyRS), incorporating the 
feedback received on your Proposal and any changes resulting fr om the detailed design. Ensure 
that the requirements are clear, measurable, and verifiable, and remain consistent with the project's 
goals and selected design approach. 
 
Appendix B: Gantt Chart 
Provide an updated version of the Gantt chart submitted with y our Proposal. The chart should 
reflect the project's current progress and remaining activities, clearly indicating task durations, 
dependencies, milestones, and team member responsibilities. 
 
Appendix C: Financial Plan 
Provide a complete budget for your pr oject, including parts, hardware, software licenses, rentals, 
services, and labor costs (based on an assumed hourly rate for team members). The Financial Plan 
must include a budget table and contingency arrangements in case funding is delayed, reduced, or 
unavailable. 
The budget should distinguish between the total estimated project cost and the actual funding 
required. Include the estimated cost of student labor to reflect the overall project cost, but do not 
include student labor in the amount requested from the course. Items provided at no cost through 
the course should be listed with an actual cost of $0. Where applicable, their estimated commercial 
value may be indicated separately. 
 
Budget Preparation Rules: 
1. Each student is expected to cover  the first $100 of project expenses  before requesting 
additional funding. Supervisors may also provide financial support, and limited 
departmental funding may be available. Since approved funds may be released later in the 
project, include contingency arrangements in case funding is delayed or reduced. 
2. Clearly distinguish between equipment, shared items, and contractor or service costs  
in the budget table. 
3. Identify contributions from team members, supervisors, or other sources and subtract these 
amounts from the eligible project expenses to determine the funding requested from the 
course. Student labor costs and items provided at no cost should not be included in the 
funding request. 
4. Identify any items that team members intend to keep at the end of the course. 
5. Estimate labor costs based on the tasks and time allocations in your Gantt chart, using an 
assumed hourly rate rather than an overall estimate of hours. 
6. Identify high-priority items  and describe contingency arrangements if funding is not 
available. For example: 
Priority 1 – GPS Receiver: If funding is not obtained, we will simulate GPS signals and 
adjust the Technical Solution, Work Plan, and Financial Plan accordingly. 
Notes: 
• Use a budget format similar to the sample Tables 2 and 3. 
• The process for requesting funding from the Design Centre is separate from the preparation 
and submission of this report.
